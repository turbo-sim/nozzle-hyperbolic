This chapter describes the implemented quasi-one-dimensional annular Euler model, its general equation of state, characteristic flux, finite-volume discretization and simultaneous boundary equations. It is self-contained: the source links identify the implementation of each mathematical operation. The model supports steady pseudo-time marching and backward-Euler physical-time integration on fixed curvilinear geometry.

The prototype represents axisymmetric, inviscid, adiabatic flow with a meridional velocity and a circumferential velocity. It resolves variation along one meridional midline. It does not resolve the velocity profile across the channel height, circumferential waves, viscous separation, heat transfer or detonation chemistry. General-EOS numerical capability and perfect-gas verification are separate: the numerical method uses the supplied fluid's properties, while the final appendix explains the independent perfect-gas reference.

1. Geometry and transported quantities

1.1 Midline, normal height and arclength

The fixed NURBSpy midline is $C(\xi)=(x(\xi),r(\xi))$, with positive radius $r$. Its arclength and total length are

$$ m(\xi)=\int_0^\xi \lVert C_\xi(\zeta)\rVert\,d\zeta,\qquad L=m(1),\qquad \sigma=m/L. \tag{1} $$

An independent positive scalar NURBS curve defines normal channel height $b(\sigma)$. Derivatives use the chain rule: $d/dm=\lVert C_\xi\rVert^{-1}d/d\xi$ for the midline and $b'=b_\sigma/L$ for height. Primes throughout this chapter mean $d/dm$. The area and its derivative are

$$ A=2\pi rb,\qquad A'=2\pi(r'b+rb'),\qquad (x')^2+(r')^2=1. \tag{2} $$

The coordinate $m$ always increases from inlet to outlet; $r'$ can have either sign. A radial inflow therefore has $r'<0$ while forward meridional velocity remains positive. Radial lines, straight conical midlines and circular arcs use the same curve/metric builder. Geometry is preprocessed on the host and then held fixed during state differentiation. Geometry-shape AD is outside this implementation.

1.2 State definitions

The nonlinear and reconstruction variables are $W=(p,v_m,\ell,h)^T$. Here $p$ is static pressure, $h$ is specific static enthalpy, $v_m$ is velocity along increasing $m$, and $\ell=rv_\theta$ is specific angular momentum. Define

$$ v_\theta=\ell/r,\qquad h_0=h+\tfrac12(v_m^2+v_\theta^2),\qquad E=h-p/\rho+\tfrac12(v_m^2+v_\theta^2). \tag{3} $$

The unweighted conservative density and physical meridional flux are

$$ q=\begin{bmatrix}\rho\\\rho v_m\\\rho\ell\\\rho E\end{bmatrix},\qquad f=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v_m\\\rho v_m^2+p\\\rho v_m\ell\\\rho v_m h_0\end{bmatrix}. \tag{4} $$

Thus the third conserved quantity is angular momentum. The radius used in converting a cell state is its cell radius; both states at a numerical face use that face's common radius. These choices also define storage and reference projection consistently.

1.3 Channel views and direction paths

The report visualizes the actual NURBS midline and its normal-offset walls. With $n=(-r',x')$, a cross-gap guide at fixed fraction $\eta$ uses $C_\eta=C+\eta b n$, where the walls have $\eta=\pm1/2$. The meridional plot preserves equal axial/radial scales. Revolving the walls about the axial coordinate produces the annular 3D surfaces.

For the representative midgap paths, the saved finest-grid MUSCL velocities give the kinematic angular phase

$$ \theta(m)=\theta_0+\int_0^m\frac{v_\theta(\mu)}{r(\mu)v_m(\mu)}\,d\mu, \qquad (X,Y,Z)=(x,r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta). $$

Arrows follow increasing $m$, so radial inflow points toward smaller radius. These paths illustrate the quasi-1D direction and swirl; no cross-gap velocity distribution is solved. Nonpositive meridional velocity is flagged rather than hidden by a denominator floor. The report displays the meridional channel on the left and its revolved 3D view on the right. It omits width-spline plots, control points and control polygons; the geometry itself still uses the same NURBS definitions.

2. General thermodynamics

2.1 Required EOS response

Numerical trial states are evaluated directly through fluid.get_state(jxp.HmassP_INPUTS, h, p). The interior flux requires density $\rho$, sound speed $a$ and dimensionless Gruneisen coefficient $G$. Temperature $T$ and entropy $s$ support boundary equations, reference scales and admissibility. Returned $p,h$ also permit input-coordinate consistency checks.

$$ a^2=\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial\rho}\right)_s,\qquad G=\frac1\rho\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial e}\right)_\rho,\qquad h=e+p/\rho. \tag{5} $$

For a thermodynamically consistent single-phase EOS, the derivatives used by the eigensystem are

$$ \left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial p}\right)_h=\frac{1+G}{a^2},\qquad \left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial h}\right)_p=-\frac{\rho G}{a^2},\qquad dh=T\,ds+\frac{dp}{\rho}. \tag{6} $$

The method uses these properties directly. It does not calculate a heat-capacity ratio from the fluid. Hyperbolicity requires $\rho>0$ and $a^2>0$; no positive-$G$ assumption is imposed. Energy and enthalpy reference levels can be shifted, so positivity of $e$ or $h$ is not an admissibility condition.

Native JAXProp and compatible custom JAX fluid objects retain their own implementation. Raw host Fluid and FluidMix objects are wrapped through JAXProp's callback/custom-JVP bridge, preserving the configured object and its metadata. That bridge supplies finite-difference derivatives with its own accuracy limits. Backend derivative consistency must be checked at an appropriate tolerance; wrapping an EOS does not validate a real-fluid application.

2.2 Setup and admissibility

For a subsonic inlet, reservoir $(p_0,T_0)$ can be converted once into $(h_0,s_0)$ through the same EOS. An HP-only backend can instead receive $h_0,s_0$ and reference pressure $p_0$ directly. This preparation is outside the nonlinear boundary residual.

The numerical driver requires finite states, positive pressure and forward $v_m\geq0$ in all cell/boundary states and reconstructed face states. Declared pressure/enthalpy bounds are checked before EOS evaluation. EOS checks require positive finite $\rho,a,T$, finite $G,s$, consistent returned HP coordinates and, when available, the reference-invariant Gibbs response $T(\partial s/\partial h)_p\simeq1$. This last audit detects a backend silently flooring temperature while passing the trial enthalpy through. Backend-declared consistency tolerances govern these audits; they do not alter the equations or clip states.

The public conservative-to-primitive recovery helper is for setup/postprocessing. It accepts a working direct density/internal-energy pair; otherwise it uses strictly bracketed HP recovery along $h=e+p/\rho$. Invalid direct results, including callback NaNs, trigger that fallback. Physical pressure bounds or a supplied pressure guess are required when the backend does not declare a bracket. Native direct recovery retains JIT/AD; host bracketing is outside the numerical residual.

3. Continuous equations

3.1 Conservative balance form

For fixed geometry the equations are

$$ \frac{\partial(Aq)}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial(Af)}{\partial m} =\begin{bmatrix}0\\pA'+A\rho v_\theta^2r'/r\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}. \tag{7} $$

The pressure-area term accounts for meridional wall pressure forces. The centrifugal projection has the sign of $r'$. Angular momentum and total energy have no geometric source: area expansion and changing circumferential velocity are already represented by their conservative fluxes and state definitions.

3.2 Frozen-radius conservative quasilinear form

Let $J_f=(\partial f/\partial q)_r$, $\kappa_A=A'/A$ and $\kappa_r=r'/r$. The flux also depends explicitly on radius when angular momentum is a conserved variable. At fixed $q$, changing $r$ changes the circumferential kinetic energy and therefore pressure:

$$ \left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}\right)_q=\frac{\rho Gv_\theta^2}{r},\qquad \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\right)_q =\frac{\rho Gv_\theta^2}{r}\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\0\\v_m\end{bmatrix}. \tag{8} $$

Expanding Equation 7 with this explicit dependence gives

$$ q_t+J_fq_m=-\kappa_A\begin{bmatrix}\rho v_m\\\rho v_m^2\\\rho v_m\ell\\\rho v_mh_0\end{bmatrix} +\rho v_\theta^2\kappa_r\begin{bmatrix}0\\1-G\\0\\-Gv_m\end{bmatrix}. \tag{9} $$

The $G$ terms in this frozen-radius representation are essential. They do not add physical energy production to Equation 7; they compensate for moving the explicit-radius part of the flux derivative to the right-hand side.

3.3 Primitive and material forms

With $D=\partial_t+v_m\partial_m$, continuity, momentum, angular momentum and thermodynamics imply

$$ D\rho+\rho\,\partial_m v_m=-\rho v_m\kappa_A,\quad Dv_m+\rho^{-1}\partial_mp=v_\theta^2\kappa_r,\quad D\ell=0,\quad Dh=\rho^{-1}Dp. \tag{10} $$

Using Equation 6 gives the primitive quasilinear system

$$ W_t+B W_m=g_W,\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}v_m&\rho a^2&0&0\\1/\rho&v_m&0&0\\0&0&v_m&0\\0&a^2&0&v_m\end{bmatrix},\quad g_W=\begin{bmatrix}-\rho a^2v_m\kappa_A\\v_\theta^2\kappa_r\\0\\-a^2v_m\kappa_A\end{bmatrix}. \tag{11} $$

The $G$ dependence cancels explicitly here because $\rho_p+\rho_h/\rho=1/a^2$. If $v_\theta$ is used instead of $\ell$, its material equation is $Dv_\theta=-v_mv_\theta\kappa_r$. Equation 11 uses angular momentum and therefore has no corresponding third-row source.

4. Characteristics and storage transformations

4.1 Frozen-face transverse variables

At one fixed face radius introduce $\overline W=(p,v_m,v_\theta,h)^T$, $\overline q=(\rho,\rho v_m,\rho v_\theta,\rho E)^T$ and ordinary transverse Euler flux $\overline f=(\rho v_m,\rho v_m^2+p,\rho v_mv_\theta,\rho v_mh_0)^T$. The transformations are

$$ D_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1/r,1),\quad T_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,r,1),\quad \overline W=D_rW,\quad q=T_r\overline q,\quad f=T_r\overline f. \tag{12} $$

All transformations in this section hold radius fixed. With $\rho_p=(1+G)/a^2$ and $\rho_h=-\rho G/a^2$, the storage Jacobian is

$$ C=\frac{\partial\overline q}{\partial\overline W}= \begin{bmatrix} \rho_p&0&0&\rho_h\\ v_m\rho_p&\rho&0&v_m\rho_h\\ v_\theta\rho_p&0&\rho&v_\theta\rho_h\\ h_0\rho_p-1&\rho v_m&\rho v_\theta&h_0\rho_h+\rho \end{bmatrix}. \tag{13} $$

Writing $\chi=\tfrac12(v_m^2+v_\theta^2)-h$, its inverse is

$$ C^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix} a^2+G\chi&-Gv_m&-Gv_\theta&G\\ -v_m/\rho&1/\rho&0&0\\ -v_\theta/\rho&0&1/\rho&0\\ [a^2+(1+G)\chi]/\rho&-(1+G)v_m/\rho&-(1+G)v_\theta/\rho&(1+G)/\rho \end{bmatrix}. \tag{14} $$

The angular storage Jacobian is $\partial q/\partial W=T_rCD_r$. Transformation through storage remains regular at rest and at a sonic state. Inverting a flux Jacobian would introduce singularities when a characteristic speed vanishes; the implementation does not do that.

4.2 Eigenbasis and projectors

The characteristic speeds, in implementation order, are

$$ \lambda_+=v_m+a,\qquad\lambda_-=v_m-a,\qquad\lambda_s=v_m,\qquad\lambda_\theta=v_m. \tag{15} $$

For the primitive transverse system the right eigenvectors and dual left eigenvectors are

$$ R_p=\begin{bmatrix}\rho a&-\rho a&0&0\\1&1&0&0\\0&0&0&1\\a&-a&1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad L_p=\begin{bmatrix}1/(2\rho a)&1/2&0&0\\-1/(2\rho a)&1/2&0&0\\-1/\rho&0&0&1\\0&0&1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad L_pR_p=I. \tag{16} $$

The two equal-speed waves carry entropy and transverse velocity. Let $r_k$ be column $k$ of $R_p$ and $l_k$ row $k$ of $L_p$. The conservative transverse projectors and angular flux Jacobian are

$$ P_k=(Cr_k)(l_kC^{-1}),\qquad \sum_kP_k=I,\qquad J_f=T_r\left(\sum_k\lambda_kP_k\right)T_r^{-1}. \tag{17} $$

The projectors remain defined when the two convective speeds coincide, or when an acoustic speed becomes zero. Focused tests compare them with the independently differentiated physical flux/storage Jacobians, including rest and both sonic directions. This verifies the algebraic decomposition; it is not a nonlinear stability theorem.

4.3 Meridional and total Mach numbers

Define $M_m=v_m/a$, $M_\theta=v_\theta/a$ and $M=\sqrt{M_m^2+M_\theta^2}$. Characteristic direction and meridional choking are determined by $M_m$. A swirling flow can have $M>1$ while $M_m<1$ and still have one acoustic characteristic pointing upstream. Boundary modes and normal-shock admissibility must therefore use meridional Mach.

5. Steady invariants and radial-flow interpretation

Along a smooth, forward, inviscid, adiabatic steady branch,

$$ \dot m=A\rho v_m=\text{constant},\qquad\ell=\text{constant},\qquad h_0=\text{constant},\qquad s=\text{constant}. \tag{18} $$

Combining these invariants with meridional momentum gives

$$ (M_m^2-1)\frac{v_m'}{v_m}=\frac{A'}A+M_\theta^2\frac{r'}r. \tag{19} $$

A regular internal sonic crossing requires the right-hand side to vanish where $M_m=1$, together with a compatible smooth branch. For constant height, $A'/A=r'/r$, so its numerator becomes $(1+M_\theta^2)r'/r$. A monotone constant-height radial inflow can accelerate subsonically toward a sonic exit; it cannot smoothly cross an internal sonic point. A further internal-transonic benchmark needs a separately selected compatible geometry.

For outward constant-height flow, $r'>0$. A meridionally subsonic branch decelerates; its swirl also decreases because $v_\theta=\ell/r$. This permits smooth diffusion of a flow that is supersonic in total speed but subsonic meridionally. A meridionally supersonic branch accelerates outward according to Equation 19. Supersonic outward diffusion with a compressive jump is a different solution family.

At a stationary normal shock, upstream and downstream limits are evaluated at the same radius and area. The jump conditions are continuity of $\rho v_m$, $p+\rho v_m^2$, $h_0$ and $\ell$, with an entropy increase. Tangential velocity is consequently continuous. The relevant upstream supersonic condition is $M_{m1}>1$. The smooth entropy invariant applies separately on each side, with different entropy levels across the jump.

In a fixed-angle backpressure family, $\alpha_{in}$ stays fixed. The selected steady solution determines inlet velocity and therefore inlet $\ell$. Angular momentum is constant within each run; it is not imposed to be the same across all backpressures.

6. Characteristic numerical flux

6.1 Averaged state and sonic width

Each face receives reconstructed angular primitives $W_L,W_R$ and one common $(r_f,A_f)$. The characteristic state is their arithmetic primitive average $W_*=(W_L+W_R)/2$. Its direct HP EOS evaluation supplies $\rho_*,a_*,G_*$ and the projectors in Equation 17. The side states supply physical $\overline f_L,\overline f_R$, conservative $\overline q_L,\overline q_R$ and side characteristic speeds.

For each wave define $\Delta\lambda_k=\lambda_k(W_R)-\lambda_k(W_L)$. With selected width factor $\eta=0.1$,

$$ \delta_0=\eta a_*,\qquad d_k^+=\tfrac12\left(\Delta\lambda_k+\sqrt{\Delta\lambda_k^2+\delta_0^2}\right),\qquad \delta_k=\delta_0+\tfrac12d_k^+. \tag{20} $$

The strictly positive width keeps all evaluated divisions finite. The regularized absolute speed is the Harten-type function

$$ \phi_k=\begin{cases}(\lambda_k^2+\delta_k^2)/(2\delta_k),&|\lambda_k|<\delta_k,\\|\lambda_k|,&|\lambda_k|\geq\delta_k,\end{cases}\qquad s_k=\lambda_k/\phi_k,\quad\nu_k=\phi_k-\lambda_k^2/\phi_k. \tag{21} $$

This piecewise function is $C^1$ in speed for fixed positive width, with a discontinuous second derivative at the join. The slope limiter is also nonsmooth at some states, so the complete residual is not claimed globally smooth.

6.2 Implemented flux contraction

The shared characteristic kernel computes

$$ \widehat{\overline f}=\tfrac12(\overline f_L+\overline f_R) -\tfrac12\sum_kP_k\left[s_k(\overline f_R-\overline f_L)+\nu_k(\overline q_R-\overline q_L)\right],\qquad F_f=A_fT_{r_f}\widehat{\overline f}. \tag{22} $$

The first jump is a physical-flux jump; the second is a conservative-state jump. The extra coefficient $\nu_k$ is nonnegative. For a frozen linear system the combined characteristic viscosity is exactly $\phi_k$, including at a sonic speed. This observation does not prove nonlinear entropy stability. The arithmetic averaged state is not a Roe state, and no Roe jump identity is assumed.

Face area and the angular-momentum radius multiplier are applied once, after the transverse contraction. The explicit diagnostic Rusanov option instead uses $\widehat{\overline f}=\tfrac12[\overline f_L+\overline f_R-c_{LR}(\overline q_R-\overline q_L)]$, with $c_{LR}=\max(|v_{m,L}|+a_L,|v_{m,R}|+a_R)$. It is a selected factory option, not a hidden fallback after a characteristic solve fails.

7. Finite volumes and reconstruction

7.1 Physical cells and lumped storage

Physical faces run from $m_{1/2}=0$ to $m_{N+1/2}=L$. Representative cell positions are arithmetic arclength midpoints $m_i=(m_{i-1/2}+m_{i+1/2})/2$, with widths $\Delta m_i$. Geometric volumes are the integrals

$$ V_i=\int_{m_{i-1/2}}^{m_{i+1/2}}A(m)\,dm,\qquad Q_i(W_i)=V_iq(W_i,r_i). \tag{23} $$

Volumes use controlled quadrature split at geometry knots; they are not replaced by midpoint-area products. Storage is still a lumped nonlinear cell state at $r_i$. This separates geometric integration accuracy from the spatial approximation of the flow inside a cell.

The integrated source and right-minus-left spatial residual are

$$ S_i=\begin{bmatrix}0\\p_i(A_{i+1/2}-A_{i-1/2})+V_i\rho_i(\ell_i/r_i)^2r_i'/r_i\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad R_i=F_{i+1/2}-F_{i-1/2}-S_i. \tag{24} $$

The pressure-area contribution uses the exact face-area difference; the centrifugal contribution uses the lumped cell state and center metrics. At a uniform quiescent state with zero swirl, the face pressure flux difference cancels $p_i\Delta A_i$ exactly. This does not assert exact preservation of every nonuniform swirling equilibrium.

7.2 Constant and nonuniform MUSCL reconstruction

The augmented spatial points are the inlet face, physical midpoints and outlet face. Denote them $\mu_j$, with corresponding blocks $W_j$, for $j=0,\ldots,N+1$. Constant reconstruction uses the adjacent blocks directly. MUSCL computes componentwise physical-distance gradients and harmonic van-Leer slopes:

$$ d_j^-=(W_j-W_{j-1})/(\mu_j-\mu_{j-1}),\quad d_j^+=(W_{j+1}-W_j)/(\mu_{j+1}-\mu_j),\quad \zeta_j=\begin{cases}2d_j^-d_j^+/(d_j^-+d_j^++10^{-300}),&d_j^-d_j^+>0,\\0,&d_j^-d_j^+\leq0.\end{cases} \tag{25} $$

Boundary-block slopes are zero. The implementation uses a protected inactive denominator for safe reverse AD and retains the tiny inherited active denominator regularizer. Each side is evaluated at the actual face coordinate, $W_{f,L}=W_j+\zeta_j(m_f-\mu_j)$ and $W_{f,R}=W_{j+1}+\zeta_{j+1}(m_f-\mu_{j+1})$. No uniform-grid half-width assumption is used.

Reconstruction acts on $p,v_m,\ell,h$; $v_\theta$ follows from the common face radius. All reconstructed states participate in admissibility checks. Componentwise slope limiting alone does not establish positivity or a total-variation bound for the coupled Euler update. Smooth-region spatial reconstruction and backward-Euler temporal accuracy must also be distinguished.

8. Simultaneous algebraic boundaries

8.1 Unknowns and outgoing reference

The full unknown is $z=(W_{in},W_1,\ldots,W_N,W_{out})$, stored as (N+2,4). The two extra blocks are independent exterior traces at the physical faces. They are not finite volumes and have zero storage. The same face-flux function couples them to the reconstructed interior traces.

At least two physical cells are required. Define physical-distance extrapolation factors and primitive references

$$ \beta_L=\frac{0-m_1}{m_1-m_2},\quad E_L=W_1+\beta_L(W_1-W_2),\qquad \beta_R=\frac{L-m_N}{m_N-m_{N-1}},\quad E_R=W_N+\beta_R(W_N-W_{N-1}). \tag{26} $$

Current nearest-cell impedances are $Z_L=\rho_1a_1$ and $Z_R=\rho_Na_N$. Entropy extrapolation is performed separately as $s_{E,R}=s_N+\beta_R(s_N-s_{N-1})$; it is not entropy evaluated at $E_R$. All these quantities depend on the current full trial state and are differentiated in the same Newton system.

8.2 Subsonic inlet and outlet equations

For a forward subsonic inlet, three incoming physical data are $h_0,s_0,\alpha_{in}$; one outgoing acoustic row supplies compatibility. At $r_{in}=r(0)$ the four unscaled inlet constraints are

$$ B_{in}=\begin{bmatrix} h_{in}+\tfrac12(v_{m,in}^2+\ell_{in}^2/r_{in}^2)-h_0\\ s(h_{in},p_{in})-s_0\\ (\ell_{in}/r_{in})\cos\alpha_{in}-v_{m,in}\sin\alpha_{in}\\ p_{in}-p_{E,L}-Z_L(v_{m,in}-v_{m,E,L}) \end{bmatrix}=0. \tag{27} $$

The fixed-angle form avoids division by inlet velocity. The selected angle satisfies $|\alpha_{in}|<\pi/2$. An explicit alternative replaces the third row by $\ell_{in}-\ell_{prescribed}$; angle and angular momentum are never prescribed together. Inlet Mach or mass flow used to construct an analytical fixture is not an additional numerical inlet condition.

For a forward subsonic outlet, pressure is prescribed and three outgoing constraints are

$$ B_{out}=\begin{bmatrix} p_{out}-p_b\\ p_{out}-p_{E,R}+Z_R(v_{m,out}-v_{m,E,R})\\ s(h_{out},p_{out})-s_{E,R}\\ \ell_{out}-\ell_{E,R} \end{bmatrix}=0. \tag{28} $$

The signs of the inlet/outlet acoustic rows follow the outgoing acoustic direction at each boundary. These are local impedance/extrapolation closures, not a demonstrated exact nonreflecting boundary treatment. HP evaluation is direct at each trial trace. No pressure recovery, entropy inversion, boundary overwrite or nested nonlinear solve occurs during residual evaluation.

8.3 Supersonic modes and regime limits

A supersonic inlet prescribes all four primitives: $B_{in}=W_{in}-W_{prescribed}$. A supersonic outlet extrapolates all four: $B_{out}=W_{out}-E_R$. Its equations contain no backpressure. Regimes are explicit factory choices.

The driver audits signed boundary $M_m$. Forward subsonic modes require $0\leq M_m\leq1+\epsilon_M$ and supersonic modes require $M_m\geq1-\epsilon_M$, with implementation audit tolerance $\epsilon_M=0.002$. This tolerance is an audit convention near a sonic boundary, not automatic switching of the equation set. Reverse flow is unsupported. A physical transient that changes the selected boundary regime terminates and requires a rebuilt block.

9. Full implicit residual and scaling

9.1 Fixed reference scales

The same supplied fluid defines a finite reference HP state. For a subsonic reservoir setup it is $(h_0,p_0)$, consistent with the prepared reservoir $(p_0,T_0)$; for a supersonic inlet it is the prescribed static state. Let its density, sound speed and temperature be $\rho_*,a_*,T_*$. With $r_*=r(0)$,

$$ q_*=(\rho_*,\rho_*a_*,\rho_*r_*a_*,\rho_*a_*^2)^T,\qquad W_*^{scale}=(p_*,a_*,r_*a_*,a_*^2)^T,\qquad s_*^{scale}=a_*^2/T_*. \tag{29} $$

These are fixed positive scales. In particular $a_*^2$ is used for enthalpy differences rather than assuming a positive enthalpy reference level. Inlet rows in Equation 27 are divided by $(a_*^2,s_*^{scale},a_*,p_*)$; an angular-momentum inlet law uses $r_*a_*$ for its third row. Outlet rows in Equation 28 use $(p_*,p_*,s_*^{scale},r_*a_*)$. Supersonic primitive rows use $W_*^{scale}$.

9.2 Backward-Euler block

For timestep $\Delta t$, the complete residual has $4N+8$ rows:

$$ \mathcal F(z^{n+1};z^n,\Delta t)= \begin{bmatrix} \widehat B_{in}(z^{n+1})\\ \{[Q_i(W_i^{n+1})-Q_i(W_i^n)+\Delta t R_i(z^{n+1})]/(V_iq_*)\}_{i=1}^N\\ \widehat B_{out}(z^{n+1}) \end{bmatrix}=0. \tag{30} $$

Division of physical rows is componentwise. Boundary rows are not multiplied by $\Delta t$ and contain no previous boundary state. Only physical cells have storage. Boundary traces, EOS responses, reconstruction, numerical flux and sources all use the current iterate inside this one residual.

The independently evaluated original steady residual uses physical rows $\widehat R_i=R_i/(V_iq_*a_*/L)$ and the same scaled boundary rows. Root monitoring reports spatial RMS over all $4N$ physical components, the largest absolute scaled BC component and

$$ \epsilon_{balance}=\max\left|\frac{F_{out}-F_{in}-\sum_iS_i}{A_{in}a_*q_*}\right|. \tag{31} $$

Steady success requires admissibility, the selected boundary regime and all three measures below the original tolerance, $10^{-8}$ in the canonical verification. A small inner timestep residual or small Newton increment alone is insufficient. The plotted convergence ordinate is the maximum of these three original-root measures.

10. Newton differentiation and linear solution

The shared Newton solver differentiates Equation 30 with respect to all current unknowns and solves $J\Delta z=-\mathcal F$. There are no separately converged BC variables and no frozen EOS or impedance derivatives in this Jacobian. State/residual kernels use JAX; backend callback derivative accuracy remains a separate EOS concern.

The maximum block dependency radius is two: MUSCL interior rows reach two neighboring blocks, and the two-cell BC closure also reaches two. Four variables and five block colors therefore require up to $4(2\cdot2+1)=20$ colored JVP directions, including boundary unknowns. Tiny systems use fewer directions. Dense AD is available for independent comparison with the declared sparse dependency pattern.

The default banded solver groups two adjacent four-variable blocks into $8\times8$ superblocks and applies block-tridiagonal elimination/back substitution. It avoids forming a dense global matrix. Pivoting is local to each superblock; it does not guarantee success for every nonsingular full Jacobian. Dense/colored Jacobians, banded directions and linear defects are exposed for audits.

Backtracking trials use $z+\alpha\Delta z$, $\alpha=1,1/2,\ldots,2^{-16}$ at default settings. A trial must satisfy domain/admissibility checks before residual evaluation. Its actual full scaled residual RMS must decrease by the Armijo factor $1-10^{-4}\alpha$, or already meet the inner residual target. Exhausted trials retain the preceding state and return failure; the timestep controller then decides whether to retry.

The production factory defaults to a cap of 12 Newton corrections. The current verification campaign explicitly uses a cap of eight, as recommended by controller study. Both use residual RMS tolerance $10^{-10}$ and maximum scaled update tolerance $10^{-10}$. When both tests are enabled, both are required. Update scaling uses the fixed primitive scales in Equation 29. No invalid state is clipped and no boundary state is projected after Newton. Both initial-driver audits and reconstructed-face audits prevent invalid trial states from reaching spatial flux evaluation.

11. Steady and physical-time drivers

11.1 Shared timestep scale

Both drivers use the local acoustic travel-time estimate

$$ \Delta t=\mathrm{CFL}\min_i\frac{\Delta m_i}{|v_{m,i}|+a_i}. \tag{32} $$

This estimate uses the fastest meridional signal. A stationary acoustic characteristic at $v_m-a=0$ does not make its denominator vanish. The implicit algorithm can employ large pseudo-time CFL values, although Newton convergence and spatial accuracy are not guaranteed by that choice.

11.2 Steady pseudo-time march

Steady marching repeatedly solves Equation 30 using the previous accepted physical field as old storage. The current verification campaign uses the fixed-growth controller selected by the separate CFL investigation: initial CFL 0.2, multiplier 1.2 after every accepted timestep, multiplier 0.5 after failure, maximum CFL $10^6$, and at most 12 retries per accepted update. It retains both strict inner stopping tests and a cap of eight Newton corrections. The first dimensional timestep is computed from Equation 32, so it changes with the case and mesh; it is approximately $7.055338\times10^{-8}$ s for shock35 at 400 cells.

The shared study loop calls the unchanged production timestep and original-residual monitors; it does not replace the Newton solver, change the spatial equations or add nested boundary solves. These are steady pseudo-time settings, not a recommendation for transient temporal accuracy. The verification driver records initial timestep, every accepted/rejected attempt, inner work, CFL and the four conservation components. The production solve_steady defaults remain separate: initial CFL 20 and work-dependent growth of 1.4, its square root, or one according to actual correction count. Passing growth 1.2 to that work-based driver is therefore not equivalent to the fixed-growth policy used here.

The isolated study found that a strict cap of five could reject the first cold step even when its residual reached roundoff, because the fifth update had not yet passed the separate small-update test. The recommended cap of eight retains both tests without a residual-only acceptance exception. The recommendation was measured on shock35; this campaign tests its transfer to the other cases rather than assuming universal robustness.

The steady spatial residual need not decrease at every accepted pseudo-time step: shock formation can transiently raise it. Success is determined by the separate original-root checks in Section 9. The initial cold guess uses uniform physical pressure, enthalpy and velocity data with $\ell=r v_\theta$; it is not an analytical solution profile. Rejected attempts and termination reasons remain in the history.

11.3 Physical time

advance uses the same backward-Euler residual but accumulates actual physical time. It caps the last timestep at the requested remaining time and stops only when that time is reached or an explicit failure/step limit occurs. Initial traces must already satisfy the algebraic BCs. Accepted steps must preserve the selected boundary regime.

The optional accepted-step callback receives the new state and actual timestep, permitting the independent discrete budget

$$ Q_{final}-Q_{initial}+\sum_n\Delta t_n\left(F_{out}^{n+1}-F_{in}^{n+1}-\sum_iS_i^{n+1}\right)=0,\qquad Q=\sum_iQ_i. \tag{33} $$

This is a physical-time storage budget, distinct from a steady residual. Backward Euler is first order in time. A fixed-CFL mesh study changes spatial and temporal error together; it does not independently establish temporal order. Physical-time histories contain accepted times/steps and inner work, not an invented analytical convergence trajectory.

12. Independent verification and present limits

12.1 Cell-integrated reference and Cp

The analytical reference module imports no FVM flux, spatial residual or numerical boundary implementation. It constructs steady branches from physical invariants and performs independent volume integration,

$$ \overline q_i^{exact}=\frac1{V_i}\int_{m_{i-1/2}}^{m_{i+1/2}}q^{exact}(m)A(m)\,dm. \tag{34} $$

Quadrature splits at geometry knots and shocks, with special coordinate treatment at sonic endpoints. Plot fields use the equivalent primitive state recovered from $\overline q_i^{exact}$ at $r_i$. They are not point samples at $m_i$ or averages of primitive values. Conservative errors compare numerical $q(W_i,r_i)$ with this projected $\overline q_i^{exact}$.

The saved pressure-recovery coefficient is

$$ C_p=\frac{p-p_{in}}{p_0-p_{in}}. \tag{35} $$

The analytical curve uses its analytical inlet pressure; each numerical curve uses its own computed inlet pressure. Saved Cp curves retain this convention. For smooth cases, the finest-grid accuracy criteria are maximum Cp error below $10^{-3}$ and all four volume-weighted conservative L1 errors, normalized by $q_*$, below $10^{-3}$. Shocked cases use the separately declared location criterion of at most two cells; their field errors remain reported.

Every field comparison shows three physical trends: analytical reference, constant reconstruction and linear (MUSCL) reconstruction. All numerical line samples are retained. Constant reconstruction is dashed and linear reconstruction is solid; the dashed analytical curve is layered above those lines. Separate white-faced circular markers are layered last. The default skips ten points between markers (stride eleven); the two numerical marker sequences are offset by half a stride. At stride one both methods necessarily mark every point. Each method has its own root, BC, conservation and accuracy assessment, and failed targets remain visible.

The conservation panel plots four absolute, signed-recorded global balance components against accepted implicit updates. With fixed nonzero flux scales $F_{*,k}$, the monitored quantity is

$$ B_k=\frac{F_{out,k}-F_{in,k}-\sum_i S_{i,k}}{F_{*,k}}. $$

A different color identifies each equation; dashed and solid lines distinguish constant and linear reconstruction. Meridional momentum includes geometric and centrifugal sources, so its flux alone need not be constant. These are steady balance defects during pseudo-time convergence, rather than physical-time conserved contents. The current fixed-growth campaign records all four components at every accepted update. Earlier archived runs retained only $\max_k|B_k|$, which cannot reconstruct four component histories. The report still displays an explicit missing-data message if such older files are loaded; it never substitutes a scalar maximum or final value for a trajectory. Exact zeros are retained in downloadable data and omitted on the logarithmic plot.

12.2 What the existing evidence establishes

The canonical nine-case, four-mesh campaign contains 72 numerical solves. All 72 satisfy original spatial, BC and conservation root checks. Eleven of the 18 finest-grid analytical accuracy criteria pass: three constant-reconstruction results and eight MUSCL results. The seven failed combinations are constant reconstruction for radial outflow, radial inflow, sonic exit, conical subsonic, curved subsonic and supersonic expansion, plus MUSCL sonic exit. Lower residual does not remove these accuracy failures.

Shock-location passes do not certify the whole shock profile. Same-station fitted jump audits check compressive branches, entropy rise and Rankine-Hugoniot defects; finite fitting windows can remain contaminated by a smeared shock. The finest shock70 constant solution has a local pressure peak 26.524% above the reference maximum over the audited six-cell shock window. That is a local-window statistic, not a global-domain pressure ratio. Complete field curves and raw states are retained.

The graded radial-outflow mesh check converges both reconstructions, while the constant result still fails the Cp criterion. A compact entropy-contact test on a constant-radius channel reaches the requested physical time with forward pulse movement and decreasing density L1 error for both reconstructions. Its accepted-step backward-Euler budget defects are below $2.5\times10^{-15}$ at the saved scales. This demonstrates a real transient and its discrete conservation budget; it does not establish general nonreflecting BC behavior or all transient regimes.

Current limitations include low-Mach constant-reconstruction pressure error, sonic-exit accuracy, local characteristic shock-cell artifacts and finite Newton robustness. Reverse flow, automatic boundary-mode changes, nonclassical two-phase wave physics, global wall self-intersection detection, geometry-shape AD and heat/friction closures remain outside the verified prototype. Perfect-gas verification does not replace a future real-fluid integration and application campaign. The selected monotone radial-inflow case does not silently stand in for an unselected internal-transonic geometry.

Appendix A. Perfect-gas analytical reference only

The numerical method above contains no perfect-gas closure. This appendix defines only the independent verification oracle. For a calorically perfect gas with constants $\gamma,R$ and an enthalpy datum $h_{shift}$,

$$ p=\rho RT,\qquad c_p=\frac{\gamma R}{\gamma-1},\qquad h=c_pT+h_{shift},\qquad a^2=\gamma RT,\qquad K=p/\rho^\gamma. \tag{A1} $$

On a smooth steady branch $K,\dot m,\ell,h_0$ are constant. Density is found from the scalar equation

$$ \frac{\gamma K}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}+\frac12\left(\frac{\dot m}{A\rho}\right)^2 =h_0-h_{shift}-\frac12\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)^2. \tag{A2} $$

Its minimum occurs at $\rho_c=[\dot m^2/(\gamma K A^2)]^{1/(\gamma+1)}$, the meridional sonic state. The high-density root is meridionally subsonic and the low-density root supersonic. A bracketed root selects the requested branch and raises when it does not exist. Choosing inlet Mach to construct these reference invariants does not prescribe numerical inlet Mach.

At a normal shock, the independent reference uses upstream meridional Mach $M_{m1}$:

$$ \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_{m1}^2}{(\gamma-1)M_{m1}^2+2},\qquad \frac{p_2}{p_1}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_{m1}^2-1). \tag{A3} $$

It retains $\dot m,\ell,h_0$, adopts the higher downstream entropy through a new $K$, and follows the downstream subsonic branch. The same-radius tangential velocity is continuous. Total Mach is not substituted into these normal-shock ratios.
