This chapter develops a quasi-one-dimensional model of compressible flow through a fixed annular passage. The flow is axisymmetric and varies along one meridional midline, with meridional and circumferential velocity components. The geometry is prescribed and thermodynamic properties come from the supplied EOS. Retaining time dependence permits physical transients or pseudo-time marching toward a steady solution.

One state represents each cross section. Boundary-layer profiles, circumferential waves, separation and chemistry are not resolved. Stationary-wall shear and heat flux enter as distributed sources. The named shear_stress_wall and heat_flux_wall placeholders return zero or prescribed profiles; they are not empirical correlations. The analytical diffuser cases use zero wall inputs.

1. Conservation laws and angular momentum

Meridional arclength $m$ increases from inlet to outlet. The mean radius is $r(m)>0$, the normal height is $b(m)>0$, and $A=2\pi rb$. Fixed geometry gives $r'=\sin\phi$, where primes mean $d/dm$ and $\phi$ is the local cant angle. Define

$$ v^2=v_m^2+v_\theta^2,\qquad \cos\alpha=v_m/v,\qquad \sin\alpha=v_\theta/v. \tag{1} $$
$$ E=e+\tfrac12v^2,\qquad H=h+\tfrac12v^2=E+p/\rho,\qquad h=e+p/\rho. \tag{2} $$

1.1 Tangential-momentum form

The area-weighted mass, meridional momentum, tangential momentum and total-energy balances are

$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial(A\rho v_m)}{\partial m}=0. \tag{3} $$
$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho v_m)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial[A(\rho v_m^2+p)]}{\partial m} =pA'+A\rho\frac{v_\theta^2}{r}r' -\frac{2A}{b}\tau_w\cos\alpha . \tag{4} $$
$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho v_\theta)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial(A\rho v_m v_\theta)}{\partial m} =-A\rho\frac{v_m v_\theta}{r}r' -\frac{2A}{b}\tau_w\sin\alpha . \tag{5} $$
$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho E)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial(A\rho v_m H)}{\partial m} =\frac{2A}{b}q_w . \tag{6} $$

1.2 Product rule and angular-momentum form

Set $\ell=rv_\theta$. Multiplying the tangential equation by stationary $r(m)$ and using the product rule cancels the geometric turning term:

$$ \partial_m(rA\rho v_m v_\theta) =r\,\partial_m(A\rho v_m v_\theta)+r'A\rho v_m v_\theta. \tag{7} $$

The four equations then take the angular-momentum form implemented in production:

$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial(A\rho v_m)}{\partial m}=0. \tag{8} $$
$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho v_m)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial[A(\rho v_m^2+p)]}{\partial m} =pA'+A\rho\frac{\ell^2}{r^3}r' -\frac{2A}{b}\tau_w\cos\alpha . \tag{9} $$
$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho\ell)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial(A\rho v_m\ell)}{\partial m} =-\frac{2Ar}{b}\tau_w\sin\alpha . \tag{10} $$
$$ \frac{\partial(A\rho E)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial(A\rho v_m H)}{\partial m} =\frac{2A}{b}q_w . \tag{11} $$

Angular momentum has no geometric source. Radius times the tangential wall force supplies wall torque. With zero shear, angular momentum is transported conservatively although $v_\theta=\ell/r$ changes.

1.3 Vector notation

The area-weighted state, flux and source vectors are written in bold:

$$ \frac{\partial\mathbf U}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial\mathbf F(\mathbf U)}{\partial m} =\mathbf S(\mathbf U;m). \tag{12} $$
$$ \mathbf U=A\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\\rho v_m\\\rho\ell\\\rho E\end{pmatrix}. \tag{13} $$
$$ \mathbf F(\mathbf U)=A\begin{pmatrix}\rho v_m\\\rho v_m^2+p\\ \rho v_m\ell\\\rho v_mH\end{pmatrix}. \tag{14} $$
$$ \mathbf S(\mathbf U;m)= \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ pA'+A\rho\ell^2r'/r^3-2A\tau_w\cos\alpha/b\\ -2Ar\tau_w\sin\alpha/b\\ 2Aq_w/b \end{pmatrix}. \tag{15} $$

In $\mathbf F(\mathbf U)$, local prescribed geometry is implicit; the source displays its explicit $m$ dependence. For densities per physical volume and fluxes per area, write $\mathbf U_v=\mathbf U/A$ and $\mathbf F_v=\mathbf F/A$. These per-volume vectors are used in storage/flux evaluation; area variation remains in the flux divergence and sources. Dividing the continuous balance by area retains $A^{-1}\partial_m(A\mathbf F_v)$, not simply $\partial_m\mathbf F_v$.

1.4 Geometry, shear and heat terms

$pA'$ is the meridional wall-pressure force. The centrifugal projection $A\rho v_\theta^2r'/r$ has the sign of $r'$. These depend on the geometric metrics detailed below; changing cylindrical basis is not an empirical loss mechanism.

The shear magnitude $\tau_w\geq0$ gives drag opposite the velocity and a wall torque. Signed $q_w$ is positive into the fluid. Stationary walls exert no total-energy work: shear reduces kinetic energy through momentum while converting it into internal energy; only wall heat supplies net total energy. The wall-source section explains the prescribed-input placeholders, units and future constitutive laws.

2. Continuous quasilinear form and thermodynamics

2.1 EOS response, sound speed and Gruneisen coefficient

Write the EOS locally as $p=p(\rho,e)$ or $p=p(\rho,s)$, where $s$ is specific entropy. Define

$$ a^2=\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial\rho}\right)_s,\qquad \Gamma=\frac1\rho\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial e}\right)_\rho. \tag{16} $$

The Gibbs relation gives the entropy-pressure coupling:

$$ T\,ds=de-\frac{p}{\rho^2}\,d\rho,\qquad dh=T\,ds+\frac{dp}{\rho}. \tag{17} $$
$$ \left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial s}\right)_\rho=\rho T\Gamma, \qquad dp=a^2\,d\rho+\rho T\Gamma\,ds. \tag{18} $$

Trial numerical states use the supplied EOS at $(h,p)$. The associated derivatives are

$$ \rho_p=\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial p}\right)_h =\frac{1+\Gamma}{a^2},\qquad \rho_h=\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial h}\right)_p =-\frac{\rho\Gamma}{a^2}. \tag{19} $$

JAXProp names $\Gamma$ as G. The numerical method uses $\rho,a,G,T,s$ directly, not a heat-capacity ratio inferred from the fluid. Shifted energy/enthalpy references are allowed, so positive $h$ or $e$ is not required.

2.2 Product expansion and material balances

For shorter matrices set $u=v_m$, $w=v_\theta$, $D=\partial_t+u\partial_m$, $\kappa_A=A'/A$ and $\kappa_r=r'/r$. Expanding the conservative products and using mass balance gives

$$ D\rho+\rho\,\partial_m u=-\rho u\kappa_A. \tag{20} $$
$$ Du+\rho^{-1}\partial_m p=w^2\kappa_r-\frac{2\tau_w}{b\rho}\frac{u}{v}. \tag{21} $$
$$ D\ell=-\frac{2r\tau_w}{b\rho}\frac{w}{v}. \tag{22} $$

Total energy combined with momentum and the Gibbs relation gives

$$ Ds=\frac{2(\tau_wv+q_w)}{\rho bT}. \tag{23} $$

Shear contributes nonnegative entropy for $\tau_w\geq0$. The heat term includes entropy supplied to or removed from the fluid; it is not by itself a nonnegative heat-transfer entropy-generation rate. Separating entropy supply and irreversible transfer needs wall temperature.

Substitute Equation 18 and define $\mathbf V=(\rho,u,\ell,s)^T$. The standard continuous quasilinear system is

$$ \partial_t\mathbf V+\mathbf A_V\,\partial_m\mathbf V=\mathbf g_V. \tag{24} $$
$$ \mathbf A_V= \begin{pmatrix} u&\rho&0&0\\ a^2/\rho&u&0&T\Gamma\\ 0&0&u&0\\ 0&0&0&u \end{pmatrix}. \tag{25} $$
$$ \mathbf g_V= \begin{pmatrix} -\rho u\kappa_A\\ w^2\kappa_r-2\tau_wu/(b\rho v)\\ -2r\tau_w w/(b\rho v)\\ 2(\tau_wv+q_w)/(\rho bT) \end{pmatrix}. \tag{26} $$

The angular third variable has already absorbed geometric turning. If $w$ were used instead, its material equation would retain $-uw\kappa_r$ as well as tangential drag.

2.3 Numerical primitive basis

The solver and reconstruction use $\mathbf W=(p,u,\ell,h)^T$. Equation 19 gives $D\rho=(Dp-\rho\Gamma T Ds)/a^2$ because $\rho_p+\rho_h/\rho=1/a^2$. Substitute continuity and Equation 23 to obtain the pressure row, then use $Dh=T Ds+Dp/\rho$ for the enthalpy row. The equivalent primitive form is

$$ \partial_t\mathbf W+\mathbf B\,\partial_m\mathbf W=\mathbf g_W. \tag{27} $$
$$ \mathbf B= \begin{pmatrix} u&\rho a^2&0&0\\ 1/\rho&u&0&0\\ 0&0&u&0\\ 0&a^2&0&u \end{pmatrix}. \tag{28} $$
$$ \mathbf g_W= \begin{pmatrix} -\rho a^2u\kappa_A+2\Gamma(\tau_wv+q_w)/b\\ w^2\kappa_r-2\tau_wu/(b\rho v)\\ -2r\tau_w w/(b\rho v)\\ -a^2u\kappa_A+2(1+\Gamma)(\tau_wv+q_w)/(\rho b) \end{pmatrix}. \tag{29} $$

This derivation explains primitive coefficients. The FVM still discretizes conservative balances; it does not substitute $\mathbf g_W$ for the integrated conservative source.

3. Eigenvalue analysis and conservative projectors

3.1 Wave speeds and eigenbasis

$$ \det(\mathbf A_V-\lambda\mathbf I) =(u-\lambda)^2\big[(u-\lambda)^2-a^2\big]. \tag{30} $$
$$ \lambda_-=u-a,\qquad \lambda_\ell=\lambda_s=u,\qquad\lambda_+=u+a. \tag{31} $$

One right-eigenvector basis, ordered acoustic-minus, angular, entropy and acoustic-plus, is

$$ \mathbf r_-=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\-a\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf r_\ell=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{32} $$
$$ \mathbf r_s=\begin{pmatrix}-\rho T\Gamma/a^2\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf r_+=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\a\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{33} $$

The basis determinant is $2\rho a\ne0$. Thus $\rho>0$ and $a^2>0$ provide a complete real eigenbasis, including rest and sonic states; the repeated convective eigenvalue is not defective. Angular and entropy fields are linearly degenerate. Acoustic genuine nonlinearity additionally depends on EOS convexity and is not guaranteed for arbitrary EOS solely by positive sound-speed squared.

Although acoustic speeds depend explicitly on $u$ and $a$, swirl changes the EOS state, centrifugal forcing and sonic compatibility. Define $M_m=u/a$, $M_\theta=w/a$ and $M=\sqrt{M_m^2+M_\theta^2}$. Characteristic direction and stationary normal-shock admissibility use $M_m$. Total Mach can exceed one while a meridional acoustic wave still travels upstream.

3.2 Frozen-face storage transformation

Both face traces use the same radius and area. Define transverse primitives $\overline{\mathbf W}=(p,u,w,h)^T$ and densities $\overline{\mathbf U}_v=(\rho,\rho u,\rho w,\rho E)^T$. The angular transformations are

$$ \mathbf T_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,r,1),\qquad \mathbf D_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1/r,1). \tag{34} $$
$$ \overline{\mathbf W}=\mathbf D_r\mathbf W,\qquad \mathbf U_v=\mathbf T_r\overline{\mathbf U}_v,\qquad \mathbf F_v=\mathbf T_r\overline{\mathbf F}_v. \tag{35} $$

The per-volume storage Jacobian uses the derivatives in Equation 19:

$$ \mathbf C=\frac{\partial\overline{\mathbf U}_v}{\partial\overline{\mathbf W}} =\begin{pmatrix} \rho_p&0&0&\rho_h\\ u\rho_p&\rho&0&u\rho_h\\ w\rho_p&0&\rho&w\rho_h\\ H\rho_p-1&\rho u&\rho w&H\rho_h+\rho \end{pmatrix}. \tag{36} $$

With $\chi=\tfrac12(u^2+w^2)-h$, the inverse is

$$ \mathbf C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} a^2+\Gamma\chi&-\Gamma u&-\Gamma w&\Gamma\\ -u/\rho&1/\rho&0&0\\ -w/\rho&0&1/\rho&0\\ [a^2+(1+\Gamma)\chi]/\rho&-(1+\Gamma)u/\rho&-(1+\Gamma)w/\rho&(1+\Gamma)/\rho \end{pmatrix}. \tag{37} $$

The implementation orders transverse primitive eigenvectors as $(u+a,u-a,u,u)$, with entropy and swirl last:

$$ \mathbf R_p=\begin{pmatrix} \rho a&-\rho a&0&0\\1&1&0&0\\ 0&0&0&1\\a&-a&1&0 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{38} $$
$$ \mathbf L_p=\begin{pmatrix} 1/(2\rho a)&1/2&0&0\\ -1/(2\rho a)&1/2&0&0\\ -1/\rho&0&0&1\\0&0&1&0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf L_p\mathbf R_p=\mathbf I. \tag{39} $$

For its columns $\mathbf r_k$ and dual rows $\mathbf l_k$,

$$ \mathbf P_k=(\mathbf C\mathbf r_k)(\mathbf l_k\mathbf C^{-1}), \qquad \sum_k\mathbf P_k=\mathbf I. \tag{40} $$
$$ \left(\frac{\partial\mathbf F}{\partial\mathbf U}\right)_m =\mathbf T_r\left(\sum_k\lambda_k\mathbf P_k\right)\mathbf T_r^{-1}. \tag{41} $$

The common area cancels from the frozen-geometry flux/state Jacobian. Storage transformations stay regular at zero characteristic speed; no sonic flux Jacobian is inverted. Independent AD comparisons verify the algebraic decomposition, not nonlinear stability.

4. Spatial discretization and numerical flux

4.1 Physical mesh and integrated storage

Physical faces bound $N$ cells with representative arithmetic arclength midpoints $m_i$, widths $\Delta m_i$, radii $r_i$ and heights $b_i$. Controlled geometry quadrature computes volume:

$$ V_i=\int_{m_{i-1/2}}^{m_{i+1/2}}A(m)\,dm,\qquad \mathbf Q_i(\mathbf W_i)=V_i\mathbf U_v(\mathbf W_i,r_i). \tag{42} $$

$\mathbf Q_i$ is integrated cell content; $\mathbf U$ remains the area-weighted continuous state. Controlled geometry integration does not make the lumped flow field exact inside a cell. The semidiscrete balance is

$$ \frac{d\mathbf Q_i}{dt} +\widehat{\mathbf F}_{i+1/2}-\widehat{\mathbf F}_{i-1/2} =\mathbf S_i. \tag{43} $$

4.2 Integrated geometry and wall sources

The source uses the current cell state and center metrics. Let $v_i=\sqrt{v_{m,i}^2+(\ell_i/r_i)^2}$, defining zero wall-force direction at zero speed. The components are

$$ S_{i,1}=0. \tag{44} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} S_{i,2}={}&p_i(A_{i+1/2}-A_{i-1/2}) +V_i\rho_i\ell_i^2r_i'/r_i^3\\ &-\frac{2V_i}{b_i}\tau_{w,i}\frac{v_{m,i}}{v_i}. \end{aligned} \tag{45} $$
$$ S_{i,3}=-\frac{2V_ir_i}{b_i}\tau_{w,i}\frac{\ell_i/r_i}{v_i}, \qquad S_{i,4}=\frac{2V_i}{b_i}q_{w,i}. \tag{46} $$

The exact face-area difference preserves uniform-pressure rest with zero swirl and wall inputs. Centrifugal/wall terms use midpoint-type flow/metric evaluations with integrated $V_i$. Zero shear and heat give exactly zero third/fourth sources. With wall inputs, angular and energy flux differences include wall torque and heating. There is no artificial shear sink in total energy.

4.3 Constant and MUSCL reconstruction

The spatial blocks are the inlet face trace, physical cell states and outlet face trace, at actual coordinates $\mu_j$. Constant reconstruction uses adjacent blocks. MUSCL computes componentwise gradients of $\mathbf W=(p,v_m,\ell,h)^T$:

$$ \mathbf d_j^-=\frac{\mathbf W_j-\mathbf W_{j-1}}{\mu_j-\mu_{j-1}}, \qquad \mathbf d_j^+=\frac{\mathbf W_{j+1}-\mathbf W_j}{\mu_{j+1}-\mu_j}. \tag{47} $$
$$ \zeta_{j,k}=\begin{cases} 2d_{j,k}^-d_{j,k}^+/(d_{j,k}^-+d_{j,k}^++10^{-300}), &d_{j,k}^-d_{j,k}^+>0,\\ 0,&d_{j,k}^-d_{j,k}^+\leq0. \end{cases} \tag{48} $$
$$ \mathbf W_{f,L}=\mathbf W_j+\boldsymbol\zeta_j(m_f-\mu_j),\qquad \mathbf W_{f,R}=\mathbf W_{j+1}+\boldsymbol\zeta_{j+1}(m_f-\mu_{j+1}). \tag{49} $$

Boundary-block slopes are zero. Actual distances handle nonuniform grids; no uniform half-cell assumption is imposed. Protected inactive arithmetic keeps AD finite. All reconstructed states are audited for admissibility. The limiter does not alone prove positivity or a system-wide total-variation bound.

MUSCL reconstructs face flux states, not source values. Both methods call the same source functions on cell states. Their sources can differ indirectly through different converged fields. A midpoint-type source is compatible with second-order consistency on smooth flows; another quadrature would be a separate numerical change.

4.4 Characteristic flux and sonic regularization

The arithmetic average of the reconstructed angular primitives supplies $\rho_*,a_*,\Gamma_*$ and projectors. This is not a Roe average and no Roe jump identity is assumed. For each implementation-ordered wave,

$$ \Delta\lambda_k=\lambda_k(\mathbf W_R)-\lambda_k(\mathbf W_L), \qquad \delta_0=\eta a_*,\qquad\eta=0.1. \tag{50} $$
$$ d_k^+=\tfrac12\left(\Delta\lambda_k+ \sqrt{\Delta\lambda_k^2+\delta_0^2}\right),\qquad \delta_k=\delta_0+\tfrac12d_k^+. \tag{51} $$
$$ \varphi_k=\begin{cases} (\lambda_k^2+\delta_k^2)/(2\delta_k),&|\lambda_k|<\delta_k,\\ |\lambda_k|,&|\lambda_k|\geq\delta_k, \end{cases} \tag{52} $$
$$ \sigma_k=\lambda_k/\varphi_k,\qquad \nu_k=\varphi_k-\lambda_k^2/\varphi_k. \tag{53} $$

The shared characteristic contraction operates on frozen-radius transverse state and flux:

$$ \begin{aligned} \widehat{\overline{\mathbf F}}_v ={}&\tfrac12(\overline{\mathbf F}_{v,L}+\overline{\mathbf F}_{v,R})\\ &-\tfrac12\sum_k\mathbf P_k [\sigma_k\Delta\overline{\mathbf F}_v+ \nu_k\Delta\overline{\mathbf U}_v]. \end{aligned} \tag{54} $$
$$ \widehat{\mathbf F}_f =A_f\mathbf T_{r_f}\widehat{\overline{\mathbf F}}_v. \tag{55} $$

The jumps are physical flux and conservative density, respectively. Frozen-linear combined viscosity is $\varphi_k$, including sonic states. Positive width prevents evaluated zero denominators; this is not a nonlinear entropy-stability proof. The regularization is $C^1$ in speed for fixed width; higher derivatives and the limiter can be nonsmooth.

Rusanov is an explicit diagnostic option, using average transverse flux minus half $c_{LR}$ times the state jump, with $c_{LR}=\max(|v_{m,L}|+a_L,|v_{m,R}|+a_R)$. It is not a hidden fallback. Sources enter Equation 43 separately and are differentiated inside the implicit residual.

5. Simultaneous algebraic boundaries

The full unknown $\mathbf z=(\mathbf W_{\rm in},\mathbf W_1,\ldots,\mathbf W_N,\mathbf W_{\rm out})$ has shape (N+2,4). Exterior traces lie at physical faces, have zero storage and enter the current face fluxes. At least two physical cells provide outgoing references:

$$ \beta_L=\frac{-m_1}{m_1-m_2},\qquad \mathbf W_{e,L}=\mathbf W_1+\beta_L(\mathbf W_1-\mathbf W_2). \tag{56} $$
$$ \beta_R=\frac{L-m_N}{m_N-m_{N-1}},\qquad \mathbf W_{e,R}=\mathbf W_N+\beta_R(\mathbf W_N-\mathbf W_{N-1}). \tag{57} $$

The nearest-cell impedances are $Z_L=\rho_1a_1$ and $Z_R=\rho_Na_N$. Entropy is extrapolated separately, $s_{e,R}=s_N+\beta_R(s_N-s_{N-1})$, rather than evaluated at the extrapolated primitive state. All references use the current trial field and participate in the full Jacobian.

5.1 Subsonic inlet and outlet

A forward subsonic inlet supplies total enthalpy, entropy and flow angle; an outgoing acoustic row closes the fourth unknown:

$$ \mathbf B_{\rm in}= \begin{pmatrix} h_{\rm in}+\tfrac12(v_{m,\rm in}^2+\ell_{\rm in}^2/r_{\rm in}^2)-h_0\\ s(h_{\rm in},p_{\rm in})-s_0\\ (\ell_{\rm in}/r_{\rm in})\cos\alpha_{\rm in}-v_{m,\rm in}\sin\alpha_{\rm in}\\ p_{\rm in}-p_{e,L}-Z_L(v_{m,\rm in}-v_{m,e,L}) \end{pmatrix}=\mathbf0. \tag{58} $$

The fixed-angle law avoids division by velocity and requires $|\alpha_{\rm in}|<\pi/2$. An explicit alternative uses $\ell_{\rm in}-\ell_{\rm prescribed}$ as its third row. Angle and angular momentum are never imposed together. Reference inlet Mach is not an additional numerical boundary condition.

A forward subsonic outlet prescribes pressure and three outgoing rows:

$$ \mathbf B_{\rm out}= \begin{pmatrix} p_{\rm out}-p_b\\ p_{\rm out}-p_{e,R}+Z_R(v_{m,\rm out}-v_{m,e,R})\\ s(h_{\rm out},p_{\rm out})-s_{e,R}\\ \ell_{\rm out}-\ell_{e,R} \end{pmatrix}=\mathbf0. \tag{59} $$

These local impedance/extrapolation laws are not exact nonlinear invariants or proven nonreflecting boundaries. Direct HP evaluation is used at trial traces, with no nested boundary root, entropy inversion or post-Newton overwrite.

5.2 Supersonic modes and limits

A supersonic inlet prescribes all primitives. A supersonic outlet extrapolates all four and imposes no ambient pressure. Regimes are factory choices, audited using signed meridional Mach: $0\leq M_m\leq1.002$ for subsonic modes and $M_m\geq0.998$ for supersonic modes. This is a sonic audit convention, not automatic switching. Reverse flow is unsupported; a physical transient changing the selected mode requires a rebuilt boundary block.

6. Time discretization and original residuals

6.1 Fixed scales

A reference HP state from the same EOS supplies $\rho_*,a_*,T_*,p_*$. It is the prepared reservoir state for a subsonic setup or the prescribed static state for a supersonic inlet. With $r_*=r(0)$,

$$ \mathbf U_{v,*}=(\rho_*,\rho_*a_*,\rho_*r_*a_*,\rho_*a_*^2)^T. \tag{60} $$
$$ \mathbf W_*^{\rm scale}=(p_*,a_*,r_*a_*,a_*^2)^T,\qquad s_*^{\rm scale}=a_*^2/T_*. \tag{61} $$

Inlet rows use $(a_*^2,s_*^{\rm scale},a_*,p_*)$; an angular inlet law uses $r_*a_*$ for its third row. Outlet scales are $(p_*,p_*,s_*^{\rm scale},r_*a_*)$. Supersonic primitive rows use $\mathbf W_*^{\rm scale}$. Enthalpy differences use $a_*^2$, not a positive enthalpy datum.

6.2 Backward Euler

The integrated spatial balance and one backward-Euler physical row are

$$ \mathbf R_i(\mathbf z)= \widehat{\mathbf F}_{i+1/2}-\widehat{\mathbf F}_{i-1/2}-\mathbf S_i. \tag{62} $$
$$ \boldsymbol{\mathcal R}_i= \frac{\mathbf Q_i(\mathbf W_i^{n+1})-\mathbf Q_i(\mathbf W_i^n) +\Delta t\,\mathbf R_i(\mathbf z^{n+1})} {V_i\mathbf U_{v,*}}. \tag{63} $$

Division is componentwise. The complete system has $4N+8$ equations:

$$ \boldsymbol{\mathcal R}= \begin{pmatrix} \widehat{\mathbf B}_{\rm in}(\mathbf z^{n+1})\\ \{\boldsymbol{\mathcal R}_i\}_{i=1}^N\\ \widehat{\mathbf B}_{\rm out}(\mathbf z^{n+1}) \end{pmatrix}=\mathbf0. \tag{64} $$

Boundary rows contain no previous trace and are not multiplied by $\Delta t$. EOS, reconstruction, fluxes, prescribed wall-source functions and BCs all use the current iterate in this single residual. Nonzero torque and heating therefore enter both steady and physical-time calculations.

6.3 Original steady checks

The steady monitor independently evaluates the spatial equations without storage or timestep factors:

$$ \widehat{\mathbf R}_i= \frac{\mathbf R_i}{V_i\mathbf U_{v,*}a_*/L}. \tag{65} $$
$$ \mathbf B_{\rm balance}= \frac{\widehat{\mathbf F}_{\rm out}-\widehat{\mathbf F}_{\rm in} -\sum_i\mathbf S_i} {A_{\rm in}a_*\mathbf U_{v,*}}. \tag{66} $$

The archived scalar policy required spatial RMS, maximum scaled BC component and maximum absolute balance component below $10^{-8}$, plus admissibility and the intended boundary regimes. The current campaign instead uses the equation-wise initial-relative policy in Section 8.4: every active equation must reach $10^{-9}$, with separate $10^{-8}$ absolute, boundary and global safeguards. A small Newton increment alone is insufficient. The top-left residual plot is the maximum active relative equation norm, not a mixture of local residuals and global defects.

The four conservation curves display separate absolute components of Equation 66. These global flux-minus-source defects can exhibit cancellation and do not replace cellwise spatial RMS. Wall torque/heating are subtracted in their budgets. Histories use the current annular implementation, independently of the separate residual-monitoring investigation.

7. Newton differentiation and linear solution

The shared Newton algorithm differentiates the complete current residual and solves $\mathbf J\,\Delta\mathbf z=-\boldsymbol{\mathcal R}$. Boundary, EOS, reconstruction, flux and source derivatives are retained. Prescribed wall profiles are factory constants, while the named source functions receive current states and remain JAX-compatible for future constitutive laws.

Maximum block dependency radius is two: MUSCL reaches two neighbors and the two-cell BC law also reaches two blocks. Four variables with five block colors need up to 20 colored JVP directions. Dense AD remains available to audit the declared stencil.

The banded solve groups pairs of four-variable blocks into $8\times8$ superblocks, then performs block elimination/back substitution without a dense global matrix. Pivoting is local; it does not guarantee success for every nonsingular global Jacobian.

Armijo trials use $\mathbf z+\alpha\Delta\mathbf z$ with $\alpha=1,1/2,\ldots,2^{-16}$ by default. Domain/admissibility are checked before residual evaluation. A trial must decrease full scaled RMS by the factor $1-10^{-4}\alpha$, or already satisfy the inner residual tolerance. Exhausted trials retain the preceding state and fail; the controller decides whether to retry.

The production cap is 12 corrections. The study explicitly uses eight, with both inner residual RMS and fixed-scale update criteria at $10^{-10}$. Both are required. No clipping or post-Newton BC projection repairs a failed trial. Inner convergence, original steady-root convergence and analytical accuracy are separate assessments.

8. Steady, grid-sequenced and physical-time drivers

The fastest meridional signal sets the acoustic travel-time scale:

$$ \Delta t=\mathrm{CFL}\min_i\frac{\Delta m_i}{|v_{m,i}|+a_i}. \tag{67} $$

A stationary acoustic characteristic $v_m-a=0$ does not make this denominator vanish. Large implicit pseudo-time CFL values are possible, but linear stability does not prove Newton convergence, admissibility or temporal accuracy.

8.1 Steady pseudo-time

The study starts at CFL 0.2, multiplies it by 1.2 after each accepted step, halves it after failure, caps it at $10^6$ and permits 12 retries. It calls the production timestep and original spatial/BC/balance monitors. Accepted/rejected attempts, actual CFL and inner work are retained. These settings are distinct from production defaults and are not physical-time accuracy recommendations.

8.2 Coarse-to-fine initialization

The hierarchy has 50, 100, 200 and 400 physical cells, separately sequenced for constant and MUSCL. A converged coarse primitive field is transferred by bounded linear interpolation of $(p,v_m,\ell,h)$ in arclength. Independent boundary traces are transferred as guesses, not reprojected onto BCs; the target Newton solve enforces its coupled rows. CFL/controller history is reset at every level.

This is grid sequencing, not nonlinear FAS or a conservative physical-time remap. The monitored campaign resets each equation's initial residual on every mesh and requires nine orders of reduction together with the absolute safeguards described below. The earlier archived campaign used a single $10^{-8}$ spatial/BC/balance target. The coarsest state is a uniform cold guess. Analytical profiles assess fields and select boundary data, never seed the sequence. Stage boundaries remain visible when histories from different meshes are combined.

If either reconstruction fails on a mesh, both reconstructions on the next mesh restart from uniform cold physical guesses, with the failed stage's identity and hash recorded. Failures are not hidden or transferred as converged solutions. The configured stagnation check exits as a failure after 64 bitwise-identical accepted states at the maximum CFL: with unchanged state, timestep and deterministic operators, further identical attempts cannot improve the target. This changes failure detection, not the equations, tolerances or accepted trajectory before stagnation.

8.3 Physical time

advance uses the same backward-Euler residual with physical time, capping the last timestep at the requested remainder. Initial traces must satisfy algebraic BCs, and accepted steps preserve the selected regime. With total content $\mathbf Q=\sum_i\mathbf Q_i$, the callback permits an independent budget:

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbf Q_{\rm final}-\mathbf Q_{\rm initial} +\sum_n\Delta t_n\bigg( \widehat{\mathbf F}_{\rm out}^{n+1} -\widehat{\mathbf F}_{\rm in}^{n+1} -\sum_i\mathbf S_i^{n+1}\bigg)=\mathbf0 . \end{aligned} \tag{68} $$

This storage budget differs from a steady residual. Backward Euler is first order in time; fixed-CFL mesh refinement changes space and time together and does not independently establish temporal order.

8.4 Equation residuals and engineering monitors

The new steady monitor takes the original signed integrated spatial residual in each physical cell. It does not alter the residual or Jacobian solved by Newton. With fixed physical scales, define

$$ z_{i,k}= \frac{L}{a_*}\frac{r_{i,k}}{V_i U_{v,*,k}}, \qquad w_i=\frac{V_i}{\sum_jV_j}. \tag{68a} $$

Each of the four transport equations has its own volume-weighted norm. Mean absolute and maximum values are retained as diagnostics; RMS is the default stopping norm:

$$ R_{1,k}=\sum_iw_i|z_{i,k}|, \qquad R_{2,k}=\sqrt{\sum_iw_i z_{i,k}^2}, \qquad R_{\infty,k}=\max_i|z_{i,k}|. \tag{68b} $$

The initial state is sampled before the first update, at accepted-update index zero. For an equation whose initial norm exceeds the configured roundoff threshold, use

$$ \widehat R_k^{(n)}=\frac{R_k^{(n)}}{R_k^{(0)}}, \qquad D_k^{(n)}=-\log_{10}\widehat R_k^{(n)}. \tag{68c} $$

Every active equation must satisfy $\widehat R_k\leq10^{-9}$ and $R_k\leq10^{-8}$. An initially zero or roundoff-level equation uses the absolute check only; no artificial denominator is used to claim nine orders of reduction. Boundary constraints and global balances retain their separate $10^{-8}$ limits. Admissibility, finite diagnostics, selected boundary regimes and all required engineering monitors must also pass. Inner Newton tolerances and the fixed-growth CFL policy remain separate and unchanged.

YAML monitor definitions select a physical quantity, a spatial reduction and an optional stationarity requirement. The verification defaults track meridional mean Cp and outlet mass flow. The latter uses the actual numerical face flux, including reconstruction; the Cp mean uses physical cell widths. Monitors are sampled only at the initial and accepted states.

$$ \langle C_p\rangle_m= \frac{\sum_i\Delta m_i C_{p,i}}{\sum_i\Delta m_i}, \qquad \dot m_{\rm out}=\widehat F_{{\rm out},1}. \tag{68d} $$

This mean Cp is an engineering convergence quantity, distinct from the mean absolute Cp error against the analytical reference. For a monitor value $J$, a complete window of accepted states must satisfy

$$ \max_{n-W+1\leq j\leq n}J^{(j)} -\min_{n-W+1\leq j\leq n}J^{(j)} \leq\tau_{J,\rm abs}+\tau_{J,\rm rel}J_*. \tag{68e} $$

The positive reference $J_*$ is fixed in configuration. Stationarity adds a requirement when enabled; a stable integral cannot replace local equation convergence. Rejected attempts are recorded separately without copying previous-state residuals onto an unevaluated candidate. A fresh evaluation of the returned state verifies the final result without inserting an extra duplicate sample into the monitor window.

9. Geometry and wall-source details

9.1 Midline, normal height and channel views

The host geometry uses a NURBSpy midline $\mathbf C(\xi)=(x(\xi),r(\xi))$ and a positive scalar height spline in normalized arclength $\sigma=m/L$:

$$ m(\xi)=\int_0^\xi\|\mathbf C_\xi(\zeta)\|\,d\zeta,\qquad A'=2\pi(r'b+rb'). \tag{69} $$

Derivatives use $d/dm=\|\mathbf C_\xi\|^{-1}d/d\xi$ and $b'=b_\sigma/L$. Geometry satisfies $(x')^2+(r')^2=1$. Radial inflow has $r'<0$ while forward $v_m$ remains positive. Radial, conical and circular-arc channels share one metric builder. Controlled host arclength inversion/volume quadrature does not provide geometry-shape AD or global wall self-intersection detection.

With normal $\mathbf n=(-r',x')$, walls are $\mathbf C\pm b\mathbf n/2$. The meridional view preserves equal axial/radial scales. Frontal and 3D views show the same annulus and representative directions; their midgap paths use

$$ \theta(m)=\theta_0+\int_0^m \frac{v_\theta(\mu)}{r(\mu)v_m(\mu)}\,d\mu, \qquad (X,Y,Z)=(x,r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta). \tag{70} $$

These are kinematic paths from a one-dimensional velocity field, not cross-gap profiles. Nonpositive meridional velocity is flagged rather than hidden by a denominator floor.

9.2 Shear-stress placeholder

shear_stress_wall(W, r, fluid, prescribed=...) returns shear magnitude in Pa. It broadcasts prescribed data and defaults to zero. Factory tau_w is a finite nonnegative scalar or one value per physical cell, captured at construction; rebuild the solver to change it. State/radius/fluid arguments reserve the location for a future constitutive law.

A future law might use $\tau_w=C_f\rho v^2/2$ with a stated Fanning convention. Reynolds number, viscosity, roughness, transition and friction-factor correlations are not supplied by the placeholder. Drag opposes $(v_m,v_\theta)$ and torque is radius times tangential drag. At exactly zero speed the force is defined as zero with finite branch-selected AD. A prescribed nonzero constant shear magnitude is not a globally smooth physical law at rest; a predictive friction law would normally vanish with speed.

The geometric centrifugal term and prescribed shear do not constitute empirical diffusion/curvature losses, blockage or separation models.

9.3 Heat-flux placeholder

heat_flux_wall(W, r, fluid, prescribed=...) returns W/m², positive into the fluid. It broadcasts a fixed signed scalar/per-cell q_w and defaults to zero. It supplies no wall-temperature solve, heat-transfer coefficient correlation or conjugate heat-transfer model.

Steady energy gives $\rho v_mH'=2q_w/b$. Thus zero heat preserves total enthalpy even with stationary-wall drag. Entropy obeys Equation 23; with wall temperature $T_w$, the irreversible transfer contribution is proportional to $q_w(1/T-1/T_w)$.

10. Zero-wall invariants and radial interpretation

Analytical diffuser verification sets both wall inputs to zero. Along a smooth forward steady branch,

$$ \dot m=A\rho v_m=\mathrm{constant},\qquad \ell=\mathrm{constant},\qquad H=\mathrm{constant},\qquad s=\mathrm{constant}. \tag{71} $$

Meridional momentum and these invariants give

$$ (M_m^2-1)\frac{v_m'}{v_m} =\frac{A'}A+M_\theta^2\frac{r'}r. \tag{72} $$

A regular internal sonic crossing requires the right side to vanish at $M_m=1$ and a compatible branch. For constant height it is $(1+M_\theta^2)r'/r$. Monotone constant-height radial inflow can approach a sonic exit but cannot cross internally smoothly. An outward meridionally subsonic branch decelerates even if total Mach exceeds one; an outward meridionally supersonic branch accelerates. A shock followed by subsonic diffusion is a separate family.

Across a stationary shock at one radius/area, bounded distributed wall sources do not modify the infinitesimal jump conditions. Mass flux, meridional momentum flux, $H$ and $\ell$ are continuous, with a compressive entropy rise. Tangential velocity is continuous at that radius. Fixed inlet angle need not impose the same inlet $\ell$ across different backpressures.

11. Independent verification and interpretation

The reference imports no numerical flux, residual or BC implementation. It integrates angular conserved densities independently:

$$ \overline{\mathbf U}_{v,i}^{\,\mathrm{exact}} =\frac1{V_i}\int_{m_{i-1/2}}^{m_{i+1/2}} \mathbf U_v^{\,\mathrm{exact}}(m)A(m)\,dm. \tag{73} $$

Quadrature splits at knots and shocks and treats sonic endpoints separately. Plotted reference fields are primitives equivalent to the four conservative averages at the cell radius, not point samples or averages of primitives. Conservative errors use these projected densities and fixed $\mathbf U_{v,*}$ scales.

$$ C_p=\frac{p-p_{\rm in}}{p_0-p_{\rm in}}. \tag{74} $$

Each numerical coefficient uses its own computed inlet pressure; the analytical one uses its analytical inlet pressure. For each physical cell, let $\Delta m_i$ denote its meridional width. The smooth-case Cp criterion is the domain mean absolute difference:

$$ E_{C_p}=\frac{\sum_i\Delta m_i |C_{p,i}-C_{p,i}^{\mathrm{ref}}|}{\sum_i\Delta m_i} \leq0.01. \tag{75} $$

This midpoint quadrature uses physical lengths rather than volumes and prevents cancellation of positive and negative differences. Maximum Cp error remains supplementary. All four volume-weighted normalized conservative L1 errors must remain below $10^{-3}$. Shock locations may deviate by at most two cells; that target does not certify the entire shock profile.

The results page compares analytical, constant and MUSCL lines for pressure, density, flow angle, meridional Mach, tangential Mach and Mach magnitude, without markers. Flow angle is $\alpha=\operatorname{atan2}(v_\theta,v_m)$. Residual/CFL histories and four conservation balances retain their distinct definitions. The final overview table preserves all failed targets.

Root convergence, analytical accuracy, admissibility, conservation and branch selection are separate outcomes; a smaller residual need not reduce analytical error. Sonic-exit discrepancies, shock-cell artifacts and finite nonlinear robustness remain limitations. Perfect-gas verification does not validate all real-fluid applications. Reverse flow, automatic boundary changes, nonclassical two-phase waves, empirical loss closures and chemistry remain outside scope.

Nonzero prescribed shear/heat checks separately assess source signs, kinetic-to-internal conversion and mass/torque/energy budgets. Native success is not their sole acceptance criterion. Settings, source/environment fingerprints, actual final fields and accepted/rejected histories are downloadable. Presentation rebuilds use saved data without simulations.

Appendix A. Perfect-gas verification reference

Only the independent reference uses constant $\gamma,R$ and enthalpy datum $h_{\mathrm{shift}}$:

$$ p=\rho RT,\qquad c_p=\frac{\gamma R}{\gamma-1},\qquad h=c_pT+h_{\mathrm{shift}},\qquad a^2=\gamma RT. \tag{A1} $$

With zero wall inputs, $K=p/\rho^\gamma$, $\dot m$, $\ell$ and $H$ are constant on each smooth branch. The bracketed density root satisfies

$$ \frac{\gamma K}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1} +\frac12\left(\frac{\dot m}{A\rho}\right)^2 =H-h_{\mathrm{shift}}-\frac12\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)^2. \tag{A2} $$

The residual minimum is at $\rho_c=[\dot m^2/(\gamma KA^2)]^{1/(\gamma+1)}$. High-density and low-density roots are meridionally subsonic and supersonic respectively. A strict bracket raises when the requested branch does not exist. Inlet Mach selects reference invariants, not an extra numerical BC.

$$ \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_{m1}^2}{(\gamma-1)M_{m1}^2+2},\qquad \frac{p_2}{p_1}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_{m1}^2-1). \tag{A3} $$

The shock reference retains $\dot m,\ell,H$, adopts downstream entropy through a new $K$, and follows the subsonic branch. Total Mach is not substituted into these ratios.
