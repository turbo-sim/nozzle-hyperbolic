Abstract

A descending backpressure-continuation study is performed for inviscid, adiabatic perfect-gas flow through a symmetric converging-diverging nozzle. The unchanged implicit Euler solver is evaluated at 87 continuation points, 14 independent simple starts and three supersonic restarts. All 104 saved states meet the original spatial-residual criterion. Comparison with analytical solutions nevertheless identifies delayed choking, persistent outlet shock capture and multiple discrete steady states at identical imposed backpressure. Residual convergence, conservation and physical branch selection must therefore be assessed independently.

1

Physical model and numerical method

Investigation objective

The existing five-case verification baseline is extended to an operating-pressure map. The objective is to quantify the solver response near choking and shock departure, and to establish when the implemented outlet condition ceases to transmit ambient-pressure information into the discrete equations.

The principal diagnostics are mass-flow saturation, shock location, last-interior thermodynamic state, entropy-flux rise and sensitivity to numerical initialization.

Continuation protocol

Starting from a uniform stagnant reservoir state, backpressure decreases from pb/p0 = 1 to 0.02. Each point starts from the previous converged numerical field. Analytical profiles and shock positions are used only as references.

The study contains 87 descending points, 14 independent simple starts, and three supersonic-restart probes. Each point is a separate steady pseudo-time solve; this is not a time-accurate pressure ramp.

Analytical operating regimes

The perfect-gas reference combines the isentropic area–Mach relation with normal-shock conservation. The exit has both a subsonic and a supersonic area–Mach root. These give different pressure landmarks.

A/A* = (1/M) { [2/(γ + 1)] [1 + (γ − 1)M²/2] }(γ + 1)/[2(γ − 1)]
p/p0 = [1 + (γ − 1)M²/2]−γ/(γ − 1)
Landmarkpb/p0BackpressureIdeal physical interpretation
First critical / choking0.93711174187.422 kPaSonic throat, subsonic limiting downstream branch
Normal shock at exit0.51314809102.630 kPaSupersonic expansion inside; normal shock on the exit plane
Adapted / design0.0937388318.748 kPaSupersonic exit static pressure matches ambient

For decreasing backpressure, the ideal solution progresses from rest to entirely subsonic flow, then to a choked throat with a normal shock in the divergent section. The shock moves downstream and reaches the exit at the second landmark. Between the shock-at-exit and adapted pressures, the nozzle interior is supersonic and the discharged jet is overexpanded. Below the adapted pressure the jet is underexpanded.

Interior-domain reference

Once the normal shock is outside the nozzle, the ideal internal supersonic solution is independent of ambient pressure. The adapted condition does not introduce a new internal branch. External compression/expansion waves, viscous separation and jet shock cells are outside the modeled domain.

The sonic throat static pressure is different from these exit/backpressure landmarks. The familiar sonic pressure ratio near 0.528 is not the first choking backpressure of this converging-diverging nozzle.

Entropy-flux diagnostic

ΔṠint = ṁmean face [s(xlast interior) − s(xfirst interior)]

For a converged, nearly mass-conservative field, this quantity estimates entropy production in the sampled interval. A shock between the last interior point and the exit is excluded. The analytical shock loss including the exit plane uses a different integration-domain convention. The small shock-free numerical value, approximately −0.00228 W/K, is a discretization error; the inviscid isentropic reference is zero.

4

Choking and shock-departure transitions

Weak driving and delayed choking

At pb/p0 = 0.9999, the computed mass flow is 0.0013560 kg/s versus 0.0028914 kg/s analytically, a 53.1% relative deficit at small absolute flow. At 0.97 the deficit is 3.52%.

At the analytical first critical ratio 0.93711174, numerical throat Mach is 0.8464 and mass flow is 2.23% low. The descending numerical solution reaches its choked plateau between 0.9342 and 0.9340.

Outlet shock retention

At the analytical shock-at-exit ratio 0.51314809, last-interior Mach remains 0.5421. A captured shock persists near the outlet even as imposed pressure is reduced much further.

At a pressure ratio of 0.425, last-interior pressure has risen to 0.65746 p0. The descending path becomes supersonic only between 0.425 and 0.420. This delay exceeds a one-point exit-sampling offset.

Mach-dependent outlet closure and branch selection

The source outlet computes a pressure-imposition weight from the last-interior Mach:

w = ½ [1 + tanh((1 − |Mlast|) / 0.004)]
pghost = w pb + (1 − w)(2plast − pprevious)

For subsonic outflow, w ≈ 1 and ambient pressure enters the ghost state. For supersonic outflow, w is zero to floating-point precision and the outlet extrapolates its own pressure.

Three probes restart the already-supersonic field at pressure ratios 0.48, 0.70 and 0.95. Each remains a steady root without an update. The local boundary has removed ambient pressure from the equations; it cannot initiate an incoming shock from an exterior domain that is absent.

This agrees with small-disturbance supersonic outflow causality, but does not establish the correct operating branch after a large ambient-pressure increase. It is not evidence of physical hysteresis. Adding the same boundary equations to a larger Newton vector would retain these roots; algebraic coupling alone would not supply exterior interaction.

7

Spatial-residual convergence

Let Ri,k denote the integrated steady finite-volume residual in interior cell i and conservation equation k. The independent residual audit uses fixed reservoir scales:

εR = { [1/(3Nint)] Σi,k (Ri,k/Di,k)² }1/2,   Di = Aiρ0a0(1, a0, h0)

The convergence requirement is εR ≤ 10−8 together with the source native SSR criterion for non-stagnant states. Native SSR uses a state-dependent conservative-variable normalization; εR remains defined at zero momentum. Neither metric measures discretization error against the analytical solution.

The largest independently recomputed fixed-scale spatial RMS is 9.78 × 10−9. The histories below show actual accepted updates at selected operating pressures, with rejected trials retained in the embedded raw results.

Zero updates has a precise meaning. Twenty-one descending points are already steady at their initial field: the initial stagnant equilibrium and subsequent pressure changes on the supersonic-outlet branch. The three deliberate supersonic restarts also require no update.

The controller rejects unsuitable trials. There are 724 rejected trial updates across the study. No point exhausts the final safety policy. These are steady parameter-continuation experiments, not a repeated timing benchmark or a physical transient clock.

8

Conservation and reference errors

Attribution limits

Pressure overshoots, large local entropy decreases and total-enthalpy errors demonstrate a discrete outlet/shock interaction. This experiment does not isolate the contribution of ghost extrapolation, MUSCL near the boundary, FVS dissipation or mesh resolution. No source modification or numerical repair is part of this report.

Limitations and reproducibility

Numerical scope

The experiment reuses the source implicit kernel, reconstruction, flux and boundary maps. Backpressure is passed dynamically through a JIT wrapper; its normalized one-step difference from an ordinary static-pressure source factory is 4.09 × 10−17.

The experimental outer controller requires both native SSR and the independent spatial RMS to meet 10−8, except at exact rest. It stops if all nine trial updates are rejected and does not use the production driver's plateau exit. Source hashes are unchanged.

Physical scope

The model contains neither an exterior jet nor viscous boundary layers. It cannot resolve oblique shocks, expansion fans, separation or exterior shock cells. The descending and restart results do not establish physical hysteresis.

A useful next investigation would vary mesh and reconstruction around the outlet while keeping this physical setup and the branch/entropy audits fixed. Alternative boundary laws and exterior interaction are separate modeling questions.

Reference checks and dataset provenance

The extended perfect-gas reference agrees with the shared subsonic reference to approximately 2.05 × 10−14 in Mach, and with the existing general-EOS shocked reference to 3.84 × 10−9 in Mach. The saved p, T and M fields were independently reconstructed to re-evaluate the original spatial residual and numerical face mass flux for all 104 states.
