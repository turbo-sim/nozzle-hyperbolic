Transient flow with coupled and segregated boundaries

Computed Sod and nozzle startup flows, with the same mesh, physical model, flux and CFL. Compare forward Euler, SSPRK3 and the two backward-Euler boundary treatments over physical time.

__NAVIGATION__

Every line uses a saved computed time frame. Playback does not interpolate between solutions. Axes use the full evolution unless current-frame scaling is selected. The sixth panel shows fixed geometry and endpoint/conservation diagnostics.

__ANIMATIONS__

One physical boundary law, two implicit strategies

Boundary traces have no stored mass, momentum or energy. Their algebraic equations are B(Wtrace, Wcells, t) = 0. The model owns these equations; the numerical method chooses how to solve them.

Coupled backward Euler

The nonlinear solve includes physical cells and endpoint traces together. Cell equations advance conservative storage; the endpoint equations remain unweighted algebraic constraints.

Segregated backward Euler

Each residual/trial evaluation solves the endpoint equations nonlinearly for the current cell states. The reduced cell Jacobian includes the implicit derivative of that solved trace. This imposes the same backward-Euler equations and does not lag or freeze the boundary.

Q(Wn+1) − Q(Wn) + Δt R(Wn+1) = 0
B(Wtracen+1, Wcellsn+1, tn+1) = 0

Forward Euler and SSPRK3 complete the same nonlinear trace equations at each explicit stage time, then recover model variables from the advanced conservative storage. Coupled boundary treatment is unavailable for explicit stages because no joint implicit interior solve exists.

What to compare

  • Coupled versus segregated backward Euler checks two ways of solving the same discrete equations. Small differences are assessed against nonlinear tolerances and conservation defects.
  • Backward Euler is first order in physical time and adds temporal diffusion at finite CFL. Forward Euler and SSPRK3 have different time-discretization errors. Their profiles need not coincide even when all boundary equations are satisfied.
  • The first-order HLL spatial discretization also diffuses shocks and contacts. Forward Euler can appear sharper here because its temporal error partly offsets spatial diffusion. Higher temporal order does not remove first-order spatial error; this comparison does not establish general superiority of forward Euler.
  • Outer Newton iterations do not count every nested boundary solve. Single-run elapsed times include the recorded setup/compilation policy and are not a controlled performance benchmark.

The two physical problems

Sod shock tube: an initial pressure/density discontinuity creates a left rarefaction, contact and right shock. Both ends are transmissive. The reference comparison stops before waves reach the boundaries.

Nozzle startup: a fixed smooth parabolic nozzle begins at rest, with reservoir pressure 2 bar and temperature 300 K. At time zero the imposed backpressure drops to 1% of reservoir pressure. The physical finite-wave outlet law handles the large pressure jump and the transition toward outgoing supersonic information flow. It is the same physical law for all four numerical methods. The initial plot shows physical cells; endpoint traces are separate algebraic unknowns.

The finite-wave law uses the permitted perfect-gas boundary formulation. This choice is separate from coupled/segregated numerical treatment, and does not change the production default acoustic formulation. The nozzle has no independent exact transient reference. The report therefore separates conservation/boundary verification, agreement of the two implicit treatments, and the evolving physical fields.
