INTERPRETATION

Read the two timing scales separately

Grid sequencing changes the route to the fine-grid solution. It does not reduce the size of the final 400-cell Newton system. Large improvements in complete-solve time can coexist with nearly unchanged cost per correction.

HLL and Rusanov have cheaper corrections in this campaign. Their two-wave / scalar dissipation avoids the full characteristic contraction used by Stadtke. This is consistent with the measured assembly costs; it does not establish equal accuracy.

Modified Stadtke has similar measured correction cost to vanilla Stadtke. Small differences should be read against the min–max spread, rather than ranked as a reliable improvement.

The adapted nozzle already converges cheaply. Extra coarse solves can increase its total time. Sequencing is most useful here for the long shocked-annular convergence paths.

04 / PROTOCOL & EVIDENCE
What is inside each timer?
This presentation was regenerated from saved results. No CFD solve or timing was rerun.

  
Production paths
Single-grid vanilla Stadtke uses 400 cells. All other methods use 50→100→200→400 grid sequencing. Production “multigrid” is nested-grid initialization, not a defect-correction or FAS cycle.
Vanilla disables both custom corrections; modified Stadtke enables both. HLL and Rusanov ignore those flags. Constant and MUSCL Van Leer are compared separately. Local derivative reuse is always enabled internally.
Repeated complete solves
One excluded complete solve per configuration, then ten measured nozzle solves or three measured annular solves. Operators and samplers are prebound; numerical/controller state resets. Only successful numerical coarse states are prolonged.
Total time includes the hierarchy, interpolation, rejected work and cold retries. Import, binding, JIT, references, field extraction and serialization are excluded. Equation, boundary, global and model audits remain enabled; optional scalar monitors are disabled equally.
Equal-work components
Twenty synchronized samples per operation after explicit warm calls and untimed setup/validation. A fixed-work call forces twenty checked corrections with zero early-convergence tolerances at each method’s final state, using Δt = 0.2 Δtwave.
Standalone residual, Jacobian, preassembled linear solve and direction timings include dispatch/synchronization. Fused corrections share work: do not sum standalone costs. Different fluxes can have different final discrete states.
Interpretation limits
Before/after probes detected no known competing Python research/test jobs; ordinary OS load and clock variation remain possible. Retain sample ranges when assessing small differences.
Convergence, physical branch, analytical accuracy and state agreement are distinct. The separate 10−6 fixed-field-scale agreement check never changes solver criteria. Annular shock35 MUSCL is flagged because both initializations pass original checks but fine states differ locally.
