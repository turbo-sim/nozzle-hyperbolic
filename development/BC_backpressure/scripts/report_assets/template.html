How to read this study

The target operating point contains an internal shock. Stagnant starts begin at uniform reservoir pressure and enthalpy with zero normal velocity. Adapted starts come from a separately solved numerical expansion and test whether the backpressure can drive shock entry. Finer meshes use the preceding successful target-grid solution. The final assessment is on 200 cells; 50 and 100 cells provide initialization and additional evidence.

02 / COMPARE SAVED SOLUTIONS

Case explorer

Failed or stopped runs display their actual last saved state with an explicit status label. The continuous reference is independent of the FVM solve. Raw quality has a mixture interpretation only inside the equilibrium two-phase region.

Residual, boundary and conservation curves use accepted states. Rejected timestep attempts are shown separately at their attempted accepted-step index; they are not plotted as successful outer updates. No wall-clock trajectory is inferred from stage timing.

Convergence requires the original equations.

Every conservation equation must satisfy its fixed-scale absolute or relative stopping test, with separate boundary and global-conservation checks. Large CFL and small updates are not convergence certificates. Relative norms restart on every mesh; comparisons between meshes should also inspect the fixed-scale curves.

Grid sequencing is initialization acceleration.

The 50-cell target starts from the selected physical state. A converged target state is transferred to the next grid by the native driver. Failed transfers, skipped stages or cold restarts remain explicitly recorded. The numerical adapted seed is separate from target-grid convergence and its cost is not hidden. These single-run, compile-inclusive timings do not establish a flux or boundary-condition speed ranking.

Only pairs present in the current top-menu selection and independently verified on both sides are compared. Pressure and normal-Mach differences use corresponding saved physical cell centers. The fixed pressure scale is the declared errors.p.scale, shared by the two records. Mean face-flow gap is |ṁ₁ − ṁ₂| / [(|ṁ₁| + |ṁ₂|)/2], using audit.conservation.face_mass_flow_mean. An unavailable pair is not assigned zero difference. A small pair gap establishes agreement between numerical roots, independently of the shock diagnostics and analytical error. A small shock-position shift can produce a large pointwise maximum gap localized near the shock; read these maxima with shock location, integrated L₁ error and the profiles rather than as a uniform flow-field error.

Controlled comparison
Both outlet laws use the same target, mesh, initial state, flux, reconstruction, implicit integration, residual scales and stopping tolerances. Stadtke enables both project corrections. HLL provides an independent two-wave flux comparison.
Continuous versus discrete
Canonical analytical solutions define physical backpressures and expected profiles. Target shock locations are assessment data. Adapted-flow starts are separately converged numerical states; they do not prescribe the target shocked solution.
Bicubic CO₂
All real-gas flow properties use JAXProp bicubic 100 × 100 tables. No property clipping, perfect-gas substitution or density-derived replacement is used. Independent interpolation can limit thermodynamic consistency; nonlinear wave and continuous-reference accuracy must be interpreted with that limitation.
